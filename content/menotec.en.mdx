# Menotec

Menotec was an infrastructure project funded by the Norwegian Research Council
(2010&ndash;2012) with the aim of transcribing and annotating a corpus of Old Norwegian texts.
([Announcement](http://www.forskningsradet.no/prognett-infrastruktur/Artikkel/Database_of_Norwegian_texts_from_the_Middle_Ages/1253962040242) by NFR 17 Sept. 2010.) The transcribed and morphologically annotated texts have been (and will
be) published in the Medieval Nordic Text Archive, while the syntactically annotated texts have been
published in the treebank of the [PROIEL](http://proiel.github.io/)
project, as well as being made accessible through the [INESS](http://clarino.uib.no/iness) portal. The funding for the
project lasted for the three years 2010&ndash;2012, and the work is now being continued at the University of Bergen under the same name “Menotec”.

As a first step, transcriptions were made of eight central Old Norwegian law manuscripts containing approx. 480,000 words. The transcriptions were made by Anna C. Horn, and afterwards proofread by several colleagues at the University of Oslo:

- Holm perg 34 4to (hand f, ca. 1276&ndash;1300), partly by the scribe Eiríkr Þróndarson (hand f), often regarded as the codex optimus of _Landslǫg Magnúss Hákonarsonar_ (The Law-Code of Magnús Hákonarson the Law-Mender)
- AM 78 4to (ca. 1276&ndash;1300)
- AM 302 fol (ca. 1300), by the scribe Þorgeirr Hákonarson
- AM 305 fol (hand a, ca. 1300), by the scribe Þorgeirr Hákonarson (hand a)
- AM 56 4to (ca. 1300), by the scribe Þorgeirr Hákonarson
- Holm perg 30 4to (hand b, ca. 1300&ndash;1325)
- AM 60 4to (hand b, ca. 1320), used as the base codex for _Landslǫg Magnúss Hákonarsonar_ in the edition of Norges Gamle Love, vol. 2
- Upps DG 8 I (hands a and b, ca. 1300&ndash;1350)

All but AM 78 4to include _Landslǫg Magnúss Hákonarsonar_ (concluded in 1276), and most of them also contain several other law texts.


As a second step, a full linguistic annotation was made of four major
Old Norwegian manuscripts:

- The Old Norwegian Homily Book in AM 619 4to (ca. 1200&ndash;1225)
- The legendary saga of St Olaf in Upps DG 8 II (ca. 1225&ndash;1250)
- _Strengleikar_ in Upps DG 4&ndash;7 4to (ca. 1270)
- _Landslǫg Magnúss Hákonarsonar_ in the above-mentioned ms. Holm perg 34 4to (ca. 1276&ndash;1300)

These four mss. have been annotated morphologically (adding the lemma and the
grammatical form of each word) as well as syntactically. The syntactic annotation is
based on dependency analysis, as this has been developed in the PROIEL project.

The corpus of these four mss. counts approx. 200,000 words. Menotec is the first project offering a syntactic
annotation of Old Norwegian. On the [PROIEL site](http://foni.uio.no/proiel), the Old Norwegian texts will join a central Old Icelandic work,
the Poetic Edda in GKS 2365 4to (a manuscript often referred to as Codex Regius). The
Eddic poems have been annotated along the same lines as the texts in Menotec.
Furthermore, several other Early Germanic and Romance texts will be found on the PROIEL
site.

The project was led by Christian-Emil Ore at the University of Oslo, and included
several other participants at this university, Karl G. Johansson, Anna C. Horn, Signe
Laake, Kari Kinn, Dag T.T. Haug and Hanne M. Eckhoff. The University of Bergen was a partner in the project, 
and at this university, Odd Einar Haugen was leading the work of linguistic annotation. 
Also from Bergen, Fartein Th. Øverland, participated in the project, and from Iceland, 
Haraldur Bernharðsson and Eirikur Kristjánsson. The name Menotec is not actually an acronym,
but is derived from the project name Menota (which in its turn is an acronym for the
Medieval Nordic Text Archive)

Guidelines for the annotation have been published by Haugen and Øverland and are
available in parallel versions in Norwegian and English, [Retningslinjer](https://bells.uib.no/index.php/bells/issue/view/157) and [Guidelines](https://bells.uib.no/index.php/bells/issue/view/158). These guidelines
explain the conventions of the morphological and syntactic annotation and also give a
pragmatic introduction to dependency analysis for Old Norwegian, covering a wide range
of annotation problems which arose during the project.

Further information on the encoding and annotation of Medieval Nordic sources will be
found on the site of the [Medieval Nordic Text Archive](http://www.menota.org) (Menota).
The transcribed mss. can all be accessed from the [catalogue](http://clarino.uib.no/menota/catalogue/menota) 
of Menota.

After the completion of the Research Council funded Menotec project in 2012, work on the morphological and syntactic
annotation of texts have continued. In 2015, a fully annotated
version of _Pamphilus saga_ in Upps DG 4&ndash;7 (approx. 4.300 words) was published as a student contribution at the
Universty of Bergen.  The syntactic annotation of _Konungs skuggsjá_ in AM 243 b α fol (approx. 64,000 words) has now been finalised. It was funded by the University of Bergen and carried out by Eiríkur Kristjánsson, Þorsteinn Vilhjálmsson and Fartein Th. Øverland.
