<Hero>
Menota is a network of leading Nordic archives, libraries and research departments working with 
medieval texts and manuscript facsimiles. The aim of Menota is to preserve and publish medieval 
texts in digital form and to adapt and develop encoding standards necessary for this work. The 
archive will contain texts in the Nordic languages as well as in Latin. There are now 16 members of 
Menota (see bottom of the page) and new members are welcome to join the network. Since its founding 
in 2001 and until the end of 2022, Menota was led by Odd Einar Haugen at the University of Bergen. After him, Alex Speed 
Kjeldsen at the University of Copenhagen took over. Since 16 May 2025, Beeke Stegmann, Árni Magnússon 
Institute for Icelandic Studies, has been the chair.
</Hero>

<Tile title="Text Archive" image="/images/N-initial.gif">
Menota now offers almost 100 medieval Nordic texts, comprising more than 2 million words,
several of them fully lemmatised. Most are Old Icelandic or Old Norwegian,
but the collection also includes Old Swedish, Old Danish, and Latin texts from the Nordic countries.
A searchable catalogue was opened on 29 June 2007.
The texts are encoded on one or more levels, primarily diplomatic (Dipl), with some also
available in facsimile (Facs) or normalised (Norm) form. These levels are indicated in the catalogue,
and the texts can be accessed by clicking the file name.

- [Menota archive](https://clarino.uib.no/menota/catalogue/menota)
- [Other archives](/other/archives)

The archive is built in the [Corpuscle](https://clarino.uib.no/korpuskel/home) application at Clarino
Bergen Center, UiB.

See our [User guides](https://clarino.uib.no/menota/documentation/menota-user-guides) for an
explanation of the content of the archive and display of texts. Here there is also information
about the text-critical signs used and how to search the texts.
</Tile>

<Tile title="Encoding guidelines" image="/images/R-initial.gif">
_The Menota Handbook_ offers detailed advice on the encoding of medieval manuscripts in XML, all 
necessary schemas for doing this, and a full tutorial for first-time encoders. It is compatible 
with version P5 of the [TEI Guidelines](https://tei-c.org/guidelines/p5/). Odd Einar Haugen has 
been the general editor of the handbook up to an including version 3.0. So far, no less than 17 
textual scholars from Iceland, Norway, Sweden, Denmark and Germany have contributed to the 
handbook.

Several supplements to version 3 (originally conceived as version 4) are being prepared under the
general editorship of Beeke Stegmann.

- [The Menota Handbook v. 3.0](/handbook/v3) (the latest complete version, published 12 December 2019)
- [Supplements to Menotas håndbok v. 3](/handbook/v4) (in preparation)

Older versions: [The Menota Handbook from v. 1.0 onwards](/handbook).

- [Handbook contributors](/handbook/editorial-board)
</Tile>

    <Card title="Menotec" href="/en/other/menotec">
      Menotec was an infrastructure project funded by the Norwegian Research Council (2010-2012)
      with the aim of transcribing and annotating a corpus of Old Norwegian texts. The transcribed texts
      have been (and will be) published in the Medieval Nordic Text Archive.
    </Card>
    <Card title="Freestanding translations" href="/en/other/translations">
      Menota hosts freestanding translations including for those otherwise published in the archive,
      including _Jóns saga helga_ and _Niðrstigningar saga_, with the hope of more.
      The translations will be published in PDF with open CC licenses.
    </Card>
    <Card title="Parallel translations" href="/en/other/parallel-translations">
      Menota hosts parallel translations of some texts in the archive, particularly of the
      hosted charter and runic archives.
    </Card>
    <Card title="Lexicographic resources" href="/en/other/dictionaries">
      Several dictionaries of medieval Nordic languages are available on the internet.
      These are in different formats and interfaces, but represent extremely useful resources.
    </Card>
    <Card title="Special characters (MUFI)" href="https://mufi.info">
      Many texts in Menota contain a number of special characters, especially at the facsimile textual
      level. Menota strives to align with the recommendations of the Medieval Unicode Font Inititative, which offers
      an extensive overview of relevant special characters, and from which several free fonts with these
      characters can be downloaded.
    </Card>
  </Cards>

<Tile title="Depositor license">
Menota now welcomes texts from other institutions or individual researchers, but cannot presently 
offer assistance in converting texts to XML. Please contact one of the board members (see above) if 
you would like to deposit a text in the archive. We encourage potential depositors to read the 
encoding guidelines in the Menota handbook and validate the text against the Menota DTD contained 
in the handbook. Sample XML files are available for inspection and comparison. The Menota depositor 
license, which is based on a corresponding document used by the Oxford Text Archive, has parallel 
Norwegian and English text. Please note that the depositor keeps all his or her intellectual rights 
to the text after depositing it in the archive. The same text may be withdrawn at any time and also 
published in other archives and other formats at the depositor’s will (cf. section 3.4 in the 
license):

- [Depositor license](/documents/depo/1-2) - v. 1.2 (8. September 2003)
- [Depositor license](/documents/depo/1-3) - v. 1.3 (1. September 2015)
</Tile>
